# 8. Roadmap

**Proposed.** Replanned after V5 (2026-10-22): see [Replanned after V5](#replanned-after-v5). Each phase ends with a conformance transcript on QEMU *and* on a board, as in Omega's A0/A1/A2 method. Every check is mutation-tested.

**Every phase's exit also includes the [manuals](../manuals/README.md).** Before a phase is called done:
- each call it added or changed has an autodoc;
- each chapter it affected is rechecked and its *Checked against* line moved to the phase;
- anything it built that a manual marked **Planned** loses the marker.

The manuals may lag within a phase, but not across one.

Board order (**VQ19**, decided): RV2 → CIX → RK3588 → Q8B.
- **RV2** reuses Omega's facts.
- **CIX** proves the ACPI path, UEFI and Panthor early. It has no hypervisor and the cleanest ACPI.
- **RK3588** proves the FDT path on Arm and reuses the Panthor server.
- **Q8B** comes last, with the hypervisor and Qualcomm's stack.
- **K3** (VQ21) joins as soon as it's revived, as the test case for ACPI on RISC-V.

## Replanned after V5

**Proposed (2026-10-22).** The roadmap above V5 was written before §10 to §17. Read against the code at V5's end, they change the order in three ways:

1. **V6's floor isn't built.** §13's, §14's and §16's libraries are all shared libraries behind interface tables, built on §17's pools, `Span` and checked readers. None of §17 exists, nor the shared library region, and VQ6 and VQ22 are open. That is a phase of its own, small and with no hardware risk, ahead of the Workbench.
2. **The Workbench as one phase is three.** Windows (`intuition`, composition, presentation) can meet §13.6's exit on the CPU with the 8×8 font. The type engine is host-tested against its oracle and can be built alongside. The desktop needs both.
3. **The design set's robustness half is behind its feature half.** There is no `init` (VQ29) and no way to wait for a port across a restart (VQ28): clients poll. Calls to core servers have no deadline, there's no graceful `STOP` (§11 D1), and several of the review's low findings are still open (§10.8: `time_to_us`'s overflow, AArch64's ASID rollover, `nTWE`/`DZE`, the PSCI call's clobbers, `KLog` polled with interrupts off). V5's `dos` stall on the RV2 is that class of bug. These go in V6, before more servers stand on them.

Audio and the GPU's depth, which the old roadmap didn't place, come after the second board: audio needs only V6's libraries and the CIX's HDA; GPU compute and 3D need a shader pipeline nobody has, and nothing before V11 needs them. The Workbench meets its exits on CPU SIMD.

**Decisions to make** (each at the start of its phase):
- **V6, the shared region.** Build the kernel's shared RX library region now, or link interface tables statically and share later. *Leaning:* now. §14's atlas and §16's bitmaps assume libraries every process maps at one address, and the region is VQ6's answer.
- **V6, VQ22.** SIMD `mem*` chosen once at boot or through the interface table. *Leaning:* through the table, which V6 builds anyway.
- **V7, who owns a window's buffers.** `display` allocates every buffer today (`DISPLAY_ALLOC`), under its lease's DMA limit and quarantine. §13.1 and §16's `AllocBitMap` have the client allocate and share. *Leaning:* the client, through `AllocBitMap` with the display as its friend, so that the limit and the cache attribute come from the device. display holds what's lent (`area_hold_lent`, as now).
- **V8, §14.7: from scratch or on a base.** Principle 1 says clean-room (about 4–6k lines); a hardened `stb_truetype` or `libschrift` base is 2–2.5k. *Leaning:* clean-room, TrueType outlines only (VQ34's `.ttf`), with CFF after V8.
- **V10 before V11?** Audio is medium and needs no new tooling; GPU compute needs a shader pipeline. *Leaning:* as ordered.

**Not in a phase yet:** the native filesystem (VQ14a), networking (VQ15), the SMMUs and VQ26's lent buffers (the lease doesn't yet record VQ9's "unconfined"), per-queue driver tasks and MSI-X affinity, a USB hub as a node source, `rest_for_one` restarts of a bus's subtree, the K3 (VQ21), and the K1's AI instructions.
